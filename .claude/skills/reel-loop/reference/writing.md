# Writing the script

## The first two seconds decide everything
Viewers swipe within 1-2 seconds. The first scene must say or show the whole promise at once.
Offer **three opening lines**, each from a different family, and say which one you would pick:
- **Question with a twist:** "Why would anyone build stairs for ships?"
- **Impossible-sounding fact:** "This canal lifts ships 26 metres into the air."
- **Stakes / story:** "France lost 22,000 men here. The USA tried something else."
- **You-statement:** "You use this every day and never noticed."
- **Ranking / list tease:** "Three reasons the canal can't be flat. The last one is wild."
Rules: under 10 words, concrete nouns, no greeting, no "in this video", no "did you know" as the whole hook.

## Structure
Follow the style card's `structure`. A default that works for explainers:
1. Hook (0-3 s) — the promise.
2. Context — where/what, one scene.
3. Problem — the obstacle, ideally a number or a compare.
4. How it works — steps, one per sentence.
5. Twist / payoff — the thing nobody expects.
6. Outro — a question that invites comments, or "part 2".

## Pace
- Spoken pace 2.4-2.9 words per second (style card `voice.wps`). 60 seconds ≈ 140-170 words.
- One idea per scene. Scenes of 2-7 seconds. Lists, steps and maps can run longer.
- Something must change on screen at least every 2-3 seconds. Nothing should start empty.
- On-screen text: max ~60 characters per text scene, max 6 list items, labels of 1-3 words.
- If the on-screen text says the same as the narrator, that's kinetic text (captions hide automatically). Otherwise the screen shows the key word or number, not the whole sentence.

## Words
- Write for the ear: short sentences, active verbs, numbers rounded the way people say them ("about two hundred million litres").
- Write numbers in the voice line the way they should be spoken in the video's language when TTS might misread them ("twenty-six", "1914" is fine).
- Accent (`*word*`) one or two key words per scene, never whole sentences.
- No claims you can't stand behind. Round, don't invent. Put sources in the post caption if the topic needs them.
- Never copy words, characters, logos or music from a reference video.

## The script table
`CLI script <video>` prints | # | Time | Type | What you see | Words |. Show that table at the Script stop. Before voice exists, times are estimates; after `voice` they are real.

## After feedback
Change only what was asked. Keep scene ids stable so unchanged lines keep their recorded voice.
